## What is treated as current for this packet

### Adverse-action notification — current

**12 CFR 1002.9** (Regulation B, Notifications) is the live notification rule used here. Retrieved 2026-08-24 from the current eCFR page and from the CFPB “current regulation” copy.

Public-domain excerpt (12 CFR 1002.9(b)(2)):

> The statement of reasons for adverse action required by paragraph (a)(2)(i) of this section must be specific and indicate the principal reason(s) for the adverse action. Statements that the adverse action was based on the creditor's internal standards or policies or that the applicant, joint applicant, or similar party failed to achieve a qualifying score on the creditor's credit scoring system are insufficient.

Official interpretations (Supplement I, comment 9(b)(2)):

- Disclose principal reasons; more than four is not likely to be helpful.
- Reasons must relate to and accurately describe the factors actually considered or scored.
- If a credit scoring system is used, reasons must relate only to factors actually scored; a principal reason may not be omitted.
- A creditor need not describe how or why a factor adversely affected an applicant (for example, “length of residence” rather than “too short a period of residence”).

Appendix C to Part 1002 provides sample notification forms and a checklist of specific reasons. Sample-form phrasing is used in this packet only as **synthetic approved sentences**, not as a claim that Meridian has issued those notices.

CFPB’s Regulation B § 1002.9 page, retrieved 2026-08-24, is labeled **“This version is the current regulation.”** eCFR’s 1002.9 timeline shows no 2026 amendment to that section (latest listed change 29 August 2023).

### Equal Credit Opportunity Act generally — current, with a 2026 amendment elsewhere in Regulation B

ECOA/Regulation B still prohibit discrimination on prohibited bases. A **22 April 2026** CFPB final rule (91 FR 21668, effective **21 July 2026**) amended other Regulation B provisions: whether disparate-impact liability is authorized under the Act, the discouragement standard in 12 CFR 1002.4, and special-purpose credit programs. Current eCFR 1002.4 and 1002.6 retrieved 2026-08-24 include those amendments.

**This packet does not interpret that 2026 rule.** It does not authorize a production fairness claim, a disparate-impact finding, or a collection program. Captain-approved demonstration rules remain: no real protected-class collection, no proxies, synthetic offline labels only, no production fairness claim.

### CFPB algorithmic adverse-action circulars — withdrawn, not current authority

On **12 May 2025**, CFPB published *Interpretive Rules, Policy Statements, and Advisory Opinions; Withdrawal*, 90 FR 20084. Withdrawals are applicable as of 12 May 2025. The withdrawn “Other Guidance” list includes:

- Consumer Financial Protection Circular **2022-03**: Adverse action notification requirements in connection with credit decisions based on complex algorithms;
- Consumer Financial Protection Circular **2023-03**: Adverse action notification requirements and the proper use of the CFPB’s sample forms provided in Regulation B.

**Do not cite those circulars as current authority.** The live obligation for specific principal reasons remains in 12 CFR 1002.9 and the official interpretations. CFPB’s public guidance index retrieved 2026-08-24 also records the 12 May 2025 withdrawal (and an 11 April 2025 internal memorandum on use of guidance).

### Model-risk management — current Federal Reserve letter is SR 26-2, not SR 11-7

The Federal Reserve’s active SR-letter index retrieved 2026-08-24 lists **SR 26-2, “Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management,” 17 April 2026**. SR 26-2 states that it **supersedes and replaces SR 11-7** (4 April 2011) and SR 21-8 (9 April 2021). The former SR 11-7 HTML and PDF URLs returned **404** on 2026-08-24.

The SR 26-2 attachment (*Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management*, Board / FDIC / OCC, 17 April 2026) is risk-based supervisory guidance. It states it does **not** set enforceable standards or prescriptive requirements. It is cited here only as **current general governance context** (documentation, inventory, and validation as client outcomes), not as an implementation recipe and not as a claim that Meridian is a $30B banking organization within the letter’s applicability note.

### NIST AI RMF — currently published 1.0; revision noted

NIST AI RMF **1.0** (NIST AI 100-1, January 2023), DOI 10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1, remains the currently posted framework PDF retrieved 2026-08-24. NIST’s framework page states that AI RMF 1.0 **is being revised**. The framework is voluntary, non-sector-specific, and organized around Govern / Map / Measure / Manage. It is general governance context only. It is not a lending rule and not a fairness certification.

Existing law, not the 2023 statement, is the notification authority used here.
